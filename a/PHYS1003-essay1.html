Before Writing: Collect the Data

  1. Pick one bus, minibus or car ride (for example home → HKUST). It must stay above ground, so avoid the MTR (no GPS).
  2. Record it with GeoTracker (or similar) and export the GPX.
  3. Convert the GPX on the Canvas page "GPS data conversion" into a CSV of time, distance and speed.
  4. Write down the vehicle model, route number, the number of passengers on board (or an estimate), and the date and time.

  • The trip: origin → destination, vehicle type and model, distance, duration.
  • Research question: how much energy did the vehicle spend moving, compared with the fuel it burned, and what is my share?

3. Analysis (models, one equation per loss)

For each segment i (length di, average speed vi, one stop at the end):

Edrag,i=12ρCdA vi2 diEroll,i=Crrmcg diEbrake,i=12mcvi2(one stop)
Emotion=∑i(Edrag,i+Eroll,i+Ebrake,i),1 kWh=3.6×106 J
  • Define every variable once, and state the assumptions: constant speed within a segment, every stop dissipates all kinetic energy, no regenerative braking (unless the vehicle has it), no gradient (or estimate mgh from the elevation in the GPX).
  • Fuel energy: Efuel=(fuel per km)×(distance)×(energy density).

4. Results

  • Figure 1: speed vs time with the stops marked (required).
  • Table 1: per-segment di, vi, the three losses, and the totals.
  • Report the following (your own numbers):
    1. Edrag, Eroll, Ebrake and their shares of Emotion
    2. Efuel for the trip
    3. η=Emotion/Efuel (Lecture 9: a car is ≈ 22 %; compare yours)
    4. your share =Efuel/Npassengers, in kWh per person and kWh/(p·km)

5. Discussion

  • Which loss dominates, and why? Link it to your stop spacing and speed (Lecture 9: critical distance dc=mc/(ρCdA), critical speed v=2Crrmcg/(ρCdA)).
  • Compare your kWh/(p·km) with driving alone, the MTR, or walking/cycling (cite values).
  • Sensitivity: how much do the results change if the passenger count or Cd is off by ±30 %?
  • Limitations: GPS noise, average speed vs real acceleration, idling energy, air conditioning, gradient.
  • Implication for your own travel habits (required).
