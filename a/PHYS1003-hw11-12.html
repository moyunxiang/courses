Q6

You would see only a faint bluish-violet glow from mercury's visible lines. Most of the discharge output is invisible UV (253.7 nm), and the phosphor normally converts that UV to visible light.

Q7

  • (i) Yes, brighter. A larger I means more I2R heating, a hotter filament, and more radiated power.
  • (ii) Yes, the colour changes. A higher T shifts the peak to shorter λ (λmaxT= const), so the light goes from red/yellow towards white.
  • (iii) Yes, brighter. More current means more electron–hole recombinations per second, so more photons (up to the rated current).
  • (iv) No, the colour stays the same. The photon energy is fixed by the band gap, λ=hc/Eg, not by the current.
