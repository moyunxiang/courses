This is an unofficial mock in the style of L2_Quiz 1–4. Each real quiz is 5 MC questions in 6 minutes, 1 pt each, answered one at a time and locked after answering. Here there are 8 questions; Q1–6 are Laplace (Ch 5), and Q7–8 are DFT, in case the end of Ch 4 is included. The scope is inferred, not announced. Budget ≤ 1 minute per question, mental arithmetic only.

Part I · Questions

Q1 · ROC of a two-sided signal (1 pt)

x(t)=e−3tu(t)+e2tu(−t). The ROC of X(s) is:

A. Re{s}>−3 B. Re{s}<2 C. −3<Re{s}<2 D. The Laplace transform does not exist.

Q2 · Inverse transform (1 pt)

F(s)=s+5(s+1)(s+3), ROC Re{s}>−1. Then f(t)=

A. (2e−t−e−3t)u(t) B. (e−t+e−3t)u(t) C. (2e−t+e−3t)u(t) D. −(2e−t−e−3t)u(−t)

Q3 · Initial and final value (1 pt)

A right-sided f(t) has F(s)=2s+3s(s+1). (f(0+), f(∞))=

A. (0, 3) B. (2, 3) C. (3, 2) D. (2, 0)

Q4 · Time scaling (1 pt)

f(t)↔F(s)=1s+2, Re{s}>−2. The Laplace transform of f(3t) is:

A. 3s+6, Re{s}>−6 B. 13s+2, Re{s}>−23 C. 1s+6, Re{s}>−6 D. 1s+6, Re{s}>−2

Q5 · System properties (1 pt)

A causal LTI system has H(s)=s−1s2+2s+5. Which is true?

A. Unstable, because of the zero at s=1. B. Stable and over-damped. C. Stable and oscillatory (under-damped). D. Unstable and oscillatory.

Q6 · ROC after convolution (1 pt)

Right-sided signals: F1(s)=s+3s+10 (Re{s}>−10), F2(s)=1s+3 (Re{s}>−3). The ROC of ℒ{f1∗f2} is:

A. Re{s}>−3 B. Re{s}>−10 C. −10<Re{s}<−3 D. Entire s-plane.

Q7 · DFT value (1 pt)

x[n]={1, 2, 0, −1} for n=0,1,2,3 (N=4). X[1]=

A. 1+3j B. 1−j C. 1−3j D. 2

Q8 · Circular convolution (1 pt)

x[n]={1,2,3} and h[n]={1,1,0}, N=3. y[n]=x[n]⊛h[n] is:

A. {1,3,5} B. {4,3,5} C. {1,3,5,3} D. {5,3,4}

Part II · Solutions

Q1 C. The right-sided term gives Re{s}>−3 and e2tu(−t)↔−1s−2 gives Re{s}<2 ⇒ intersection −3<Re{s}<2.

Q2 A. A=−1+5−1+3=2, B=−3+5−3+1=−1; right-sided ⇒ (2e−t−e−3t)u(t).

Q3 B. f(0+)=lim⁡s→∞2s+3s+1=2. The single pole at 0 plus an LHP pole means FVT is valid: f(∞)=lim⁡s→02s+3s+1=3. Check: f=3−e−t.

Q4 C. 13F(s3)=13⋅1s/3+2=1s+6; the ROC is scaled by 3 ⇒ Re{s}>−6. Directly: f=e−2tu(t) ⇒ f(3t)=e−6tu(t).

Q5 C. Poles −1±j2 are in the LHP, the system is causal, so it is stable. The poles are complex, so it is oscillatory (under-damped). A RHP zero does not affect stability.

Q6 B. F1F2=1s+10: the pole at −3 cancels, so the ROC extends to Re{s}>−10.

Q7 C. W4=−j: X[1]=1+2(−j)+0+(−1)(j)=1−3j.

Q8 B. y[n]=x[n]+x[((n−1))3]: y[0]=1+3=4, y[1]=2+1=3, y[2]=3+2=5.
