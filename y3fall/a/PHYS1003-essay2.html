Before Writing: Collect the Data

  1. Activity log: sessions per week nw, hours per session t, playing weeks per year W (term weeks plus holidays). Take these from your court bookings or calendar over the last ~8 weeks and extrapolate. State how you extrapolated.
  2. Venue: indoor or outdoor; whether it is air-conditioned; the number and wattage of the light fittings (count them and photograph a nameplate, or ask the sports facilities office); the court and hall dimensions; how many players share the court (Ns).
  3. Body: your mass m and a MET value for basketball from the Compendium of Physical Activities (cite the activity code). A smartwatch kcal reading can serve as a cross-check.
  4. Shower after play: flow rate f (time how long it takes to fill a bucket of known volume), shower duration, cold-water and shower temperatures, and the heater type and its efficiency.
  5. Laundry: kit loads per week; washer and dryer kWh per cycle (EMSD Energy Label or the manufacturer's sheet); wash temperature.
  6. Travel: route and distance to the court, and the transport mode's kWh per passenger-km. You can reuse your own Essay 1 value for a minibus and cite your Essay 1.
  7. Equipment: the mass, main material and lifetime of your shoes, ball and jerseys. Look up embodied-energy intensities (kWh/kg) in MacKay Ch. 15 / App. H or the Bath ICE database.
  8. Consumables: drink bottles per session and the mass of one bottle (weigh it).

2. Methodology & Data Collection

  • System boundary (define it explicitly; this is a stated requirement):
    • Direct operational is energy used on court during play: your share of the venue lighting, your share of the venue AC/ventilation (if any), and your extra metabolic (food) energy.
    • Indirect is energy used before or after play, or embodied in things: travel to the court (logistics), the shower, laundering the kit, the embodied energy of the extra food (diet), the embodied energy of the equipment, drink packaging, and maintenance.
    • Excluded: construction of the building and resurfacing of the court. These are spread over very many users, have no data, and are named as a limitation.
  • Energy basis: give electricity as delivered kWh, food as its chemical kWh, and embodied energy as primary kWh. Say this once. Optionally convert electricity to primary energy with 10.7 MJ per kWhe ≈ 3 (L19 p.16), so the categories are not mixed without comment.
  • Annual scaling: every item is computed per session and then multiplied by N=nwW sessions per year. Items counted per year (equipment) are the exception.
  • Units: 1 kcal = 4184 J (L01 p.55); 1 kWh = 3.6 MJ (L19 p.4).
  • List every looked-up parameter with its citation (a symbol list in the text, or inside Table 1).

  • Lighting: count the fittings, or estimate with illuminance Ev (lux, from a sports-lighting standard) × floor area A ÷ luminous efficacy ηlm (L18 p.20, p.34: LED ≈ 138 lm/W) ÷ utilisation factor UF.
  • AC: use the nameplate power if the facilities office gives it. Otherwise use the building heat-loss model (L19 p.30, p.33) with COPcool=Qc/W (L07 p.10). Most of each player's metabolic power turns into heat in the hall (the body as a heat engine has e≤1−Tc/Th≈5.5%, L06 p.21), so Q˙internal includes about MET×m×1.16 W per player plus the lights.
  • Food: subtract 1 MET (resting), because you would burn that anyway. That is a boundary decision, so justify it.

5. Discussion

  • Interpretation: why the dominant item dominates. It is likely one of the indirect items (shower, laundry, diet), which shows why the boundary matters. Link it to the physics, e.g. Q∝ΔT for hot water and the 5× fossil input for food.
  • Operational change (no new hardware; pick the largest controllable item in Table 1), for example:
    • skip the tumble dryer and air-dry the kit, and wash in full cold loads: ΔE=nloads [Edry+(Ewash,40−Ewash,cold)]; or
    • shorten the shower from ts to ts′: ΔE=Nρf(ts−ts′)cwΔT/ηheater.
  • Technological upgrade, for example:
    • a low-flow showerhead (f→f′, e.g. ≤ 6 L/min): ΔE=Nρ(f−f′)tscwΔT/ηheater; or
    • a heat-pump water heater: E′=Q/COPheat with COPheat≤Th/(Th−Tc) (L07 p.11; a real value is ≈ 3, cite it).
    • Subtract the new device's own embodied energy and give a payback time in years (the life-cycle idea, L19 p.16–17).
  • Combined saving: apply the two measures one after the other, not added. Both scale Q, so E′′=E (ts′/ts)(f′/f). Report the kWh/yr and the % of Eyear, and add Table 2 (or Figure 2): before vs after.
  • Fun is kept: playing time and venue do not change.
  • Sensitivity: ±30 % on MET, Ns, shower flow and k. Does the ranking of the items change?
  • Limitations: MET is a table value, not a measurement; the venue power is estimated; k=5 is a UK average diet; the session count is extrapolated; the excluded building energy.
  • Implication for your own habits (one or two lines).
